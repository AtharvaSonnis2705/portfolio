## Problem

People often type how they feel before they're ready to talk to anyone. A bot can't replace a counsellor, but it can respond quickly, kindly and consistently — as long as it understands _what kind_ of message it received.

## Approach & key decisions

**Intent classification, not free generation.** Responses come from curated categories rather than a generative model, so the bot can't improvise an unsafe or made-up reply — the trade-off is less flexibility.

**A small, explainable NLP pipeline.** Messages are tokenized and lemmatized with NLTK ("worried", "worrying" → "worry"), turned into a bag-of-words vector, and classified by a dense neural network built with Keras on TensorFlow, with a softmax over intent classes.

## What I'd do next

- Replace bag-of-words with sentence embeddings (Hugging Face Transformers) for better generalization.
- Add a retrieval layer over vetted mental-health resources (RAG) with citations.
- Build an evaluation set reviewed by a professional before any real-world use.
